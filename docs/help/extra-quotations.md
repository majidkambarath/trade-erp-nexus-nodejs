# Quotations

A quotation is an offer. It moves no stock and posts nothing to the books: it exists to be sent,
accepted or refused, and then turned into a real document.

## How one travels

1. **Draft.** Add the customer, the lines and the date it is valid until. Only a draft can be
   edited.
2. **Sent.** Record that it has gone to the customer.
3. **Accepted**, or **Rejected** if they say no.
4. **Converted**, once it becomes a sales order or a delivery note. That can be done once.

## Changing one after it has gone out

A quotation that has been sent cannot be edited, because the customer is holding a copy of it.
Revise it instead: Zarvia makes a new version numbered `-R1`, and marks the first one
**superseded** straight away, so there is never a question about which offer stands. Deleting a
draft revision brings the original back.

## Expired

A sent quotation whose "valid until" day has passed reads as **expired**. Nothing changes it - it is
worked out from the date each time it is looked at - so a quotation becomes current again the moment
you extend that date on a revision.

## Turning it into something real

Converting makes either a **draft sales order** or a **delivery note**. Prices are worked out again
on the new document's own date, so an offer made last month follows whatever tax rate applies when
it is accepted.

For several deliveries against one offer, convert it to a sales order first and raise the delivery
notes from there.

## What it does not do

Marking a quotation as sent records that you sent it; it does not email the customer. Nothing is
reserved: the goods on an offer are still free stock until a sales order is approved.
