# The home dashboard

The first screen after signing in. Every figure on it is read from your own books - nothing on it is
an estimate or a sample - so a number here always has a report behind it that explains itself.

## The period, and what it is compared with

The period picker at the top sets every figure on the page. The comparison is chosen to be fair:

- a month is compared with the month before
- a quarter with the quarter before
- a year with the year before
- any other range with the same number of days immediately before it

So "up 12%" always means against a stretch of the same length.

## The tabs

- **Overview** - what was bought, what is in stock, what was sold and what has been collected, with
  what customers still owe you and what you still owe suppliers. Each tile opens the screen behind it.
- **Sales** - who is buying, what they are buying and how that has moved.
- **Inventory** - stock value, what moved it over the period, and which categories earn their margin.
- **Reports** - profit from revenue down to the bottom line, where the expenses go, and profit by month.

## Why a figure might read zero

Almost always one of three things, and each says so when you look at the report behind it:

- the period has no documents in it - check the dates
- the documents are still **drafts**: only an approved document reaches the books
- you are looking at one branch and the work was done in another

## What customers owe

This is taken from the accounts, not from the list of unpaid invoices, so money received on account
is already counted. That is why it can differ from adding up the open invoices by hand - the account
is the one that is right.

## The cash cycle

How long money is tied up: how long stock sits, plus how long customers take to pay, less how long
you take to pay suppliers. It needs at least a fortnight of trading before it means anything, and it
says so rather than showing a figure it cannot stand behind.
