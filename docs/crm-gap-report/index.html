What no competitor does the same way

The pipeline is wired to the accounts. That is the product, and the gaps below are measured against it.

It moves itself

A quotation sent, an offer accepted and an invoice approved move the deal. Each move names the document behind it, and an undone move stays on record.

Won at the real figure

A won deal takes the approved invoice's total, so the forecast and the books agree.

One set of people

A contact is the same record in the CRM and on the customer, under one id.

It will not guess

With two open deals for one customer, an invoice moves neither. A person decides.

Selling the CRM on its own

Some clients will buy the CRM and nothing else. Until 21 October that did not work.

Before

  • Qualifying a lead needed the Sales module, so it failed for a CRM-only client.
  • The new-deal form could not choose a customer without Sales access.
  • No plan sold the CRM alone.
  • The owner landed on an ERP dashboard of zeros and could not add colleagues.

Now

  • A CRM-only plan, with its own people and settings.
  • Qualifying, opening a deal and adding colleagues all work without Sales.
  • The owner lands on the CRM dashboard.
  • Import a spreadsheet, and keep files on a lead or a deal.

One limit remains. The automation reacts to quotations and invoices, which a CRM-only client does not raise, so they move deals by hand. The recommendation is to sell it as exactly that and make the automation the reason to add the ERP.

Selling in other markets

Gulf, India, Asia, Africa

Open now

The same small-business shape already served. What helps most: WhatsApp, Arabic, deal currency and tax by country. No certificate is needed to sell.

Europe and the UK

Closed for now

Needs recorded consent, erasure that reaches every related record, retention rules and EU hosting. ISO 27001 is what larger buyers ask for.

United States

Heaviest

Expects SOC 2 Type 2 and a larger feature set: forecasting, territories, custom fields and many integrations. Treat it as a decision for 2027.
